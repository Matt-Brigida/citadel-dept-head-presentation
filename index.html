[7:30 to 9:00 | 1 minute 30 seconds] This is what accountability looks like in practice. Our general business knowledge measure was the ETS Major Field Test in the capstone, with 145 out of 200 as the benchmark. In spring 2022, 68 percent met it and the mean was 151, about the 37th percentile nationally. We did not stop at the headline. Subscores showed quantitative and economics performance near the 38th percentile while every other category was above average. We took that diagnosis to the full faculty retreat on May 10, 2022. Faculty agreed to short quantitative bell-ringers, practice tools for students who needed them, and explicit review of fundamentals across courses. On the same measure in fall 2023 and spring 2024, 80 percent met the benchmark. Twelve points, the highest in our series. My point is not that the head had the answer. My responsibility was to make sure we looked honestly, acted collectively, and measured again.

[13:30 to 16:30 | 3 minutes] Several Baker School faculty have already invested in teaching with generative AI. I would not arrive to explain AI. The leadership question is how to give that work shared curricular and assessment infrastructure. If a learning objective is assessed through a memo or take-home analysis, we may no longer know whose competency we measured. That points toward a mixed assessment portfolio: more in-class performance, short oral defenses, and objectives that ask students to direct, check, and defend AI-supported work. There is also a timely accreditation question. My current understanding of the 2026 AACSB standards is that teaching effectiveness moves into Standard 3.2 on the faculty qualification axis. Faculty with teaching duties who do not meet school-defined criteria become Additional and do not count in either the 40 percent SA or 90 percent combined sufficiency ratios. With a three-year ramp-up and first formal evaluation for 2029-30 visits, the transition is when criteria should be developed, not 2029. Advisory board and employer partners could contribute authentic cases and serve as occasional audiences for oral defense, while faculty retain control of assessment design. Verify every standards detail before delivery. Frame this as an opportunity to define credible evidence together, not as a compliance alarm.

[19:00 to 21:00 | 2 minutes] These are not three initiatives to launch at once. Sequence is part of leadership. In year one, after listening, the faculty would choose the questions worth testing, define what success would look like, and run bounded pilots that fit available capacity. In year two, we would scale only what the evidence and faculty ownership support, resource it properly, and stop what did not work. External engagement is not a fourth initiative. It is the layer that can resource all three through donors, advisory participation, placements, and capstone work. The head's job is to make partnerships usable by faculty, not simply accumulate contacts.
